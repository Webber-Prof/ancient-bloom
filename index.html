The shelf

Made fresh, in batches small enough to count.

We'd rather make a few things properly than thirty things adequately. Everything here is made to be used up, not displayed.

Method

Four weeks from field to jar. Mostly waiting.

Nothing here is complicated. It's just slow, and slow is the part most brands skip.

01 — Sourced

Bought small

Certified organic where certification exists, farm-direct or wild-harvested where it doesn't. Bought in quantities we'll use within the season.

02 — Infused

Six weeks, no heat

Botanicals sit in oil in glass, away from light, turned by hand every few days. Heat would do it in an afternoon and take the good part with it.

03 — Poured

Forty at a time

Filtered twice, poured into sterilised amber glass, capped and weighed. About forty jars a batch — the most two people can do in a day without rushing.

04 — Rested

Fourteen days

Every batch sits a fortnight before it's sold, so the scent settles and anything that was going to separate has. Then it's labelled with the pour date.

The label, in plain words

What goes in, and what never does.

Full ingredient lists are printed on every jar and posted with each batch. Nothing hides behind "fragrance" or "proprietary blend".

In

  • Cold-pressed carrier oils, bought small and used within the season
  • Whole botanicals — dried, not extracted
  • Unrefined butters: kokum, mango, shea
  • Essential oils and absolutes, at low percentages
  • Amber glass and aluminium — refillable, recyclable

Out

  • Synthetic fragrance and "parfum"
  • Parabens, phthalates, sulphates
  • Mineral oil, silicones, petrolatum
  • Synthetic colour
  • Anything tested on animals, at any stage
  • Claims we can't back up on a label

Questions

The ones we're asked most.

Why does the colour change from batch to batch?

Because the plants do. Turmeric grown in a wet year is paler; rose petals dried in March are deeper than the ones dried in July. We don't correct for it with colourant, so the jar you get in October won't exactly match the one from April. The formula is identical.

How long does it keep?

Six months from the pour date printed on the base, kept out of direct sun with the lid on. If anything ever smells sharp or off, stop using it and tell us — we'll replace it.

Should I patch test?

Yes, always, with any new product. A little on the inside of your forearm, left alone for a day. Natural doesn't mean non-reactive — plenty of people react to sandalwood, nut oils or citrus. Full ingredient lists are on every jar so you can check before you buy.

Is it safe during pregnancy?

Some of the botanicals we use aren't generally recommended in pregnancy, and it depends on the product. Message us and we'll send the full ingredient list — then run it past your doctor or midwife rather than us. We're not qualified to make that call for you.

Is everything certified organic?

Not all of it, and we'd rather say so. Where a certified organic supplier exists at a scale we can buy from, we use them. Where they don't, we buy direct from growers we've visited. "Organic and natural" on our label means that, not a certificate we've bought.

Can I order in bulk, or for a wedding?

Yes. Favours, hampers and gifting are a good chunk of what we do. Give us four weeks' notice — a batch takes that long — and tell us the quantity and budget. We can do custom labels and unscented versions.

Do you take returns?

On unopened jars, within seven days, yes — you pay return postage. Once a jar's been opened we can't resell it, so we don't take it back. If something arrived broken or wrong, send us a photo and we'll fix it, no argument.
